The turn had no exit condition

A closed question was answered on the first tool call, then became an eighteen-file change proposal over ten more. Revision 1 of this document blamed the harness. That was wrong, and being wrong in that particular direction is itself the finding: the defect is that no goal state or stopping point was ever defined, and everything else — including the harness — is downstream of that. Revision 3 turns the fix into a two-step front matter: state the goal and the stop point, then run /optimize-graph once across every task the turn implies — its Stage-0 triage is the exit condition the failed turn never had.

Revision 2  What changed, and why

Two corrections were made to revision 1, both of which invert its conclusion:

  • Autopilot is orthogonal to stopping precision. It grants permission to proceed without check-ins. It says nothing about what to proceed toward or when to stop. Revision 1 collapsed those axes and made the harness the cause.
  • Autonomy is toward a goal state, and the goal state was ignored. Here the goal state was answer a precise question. “Work autonomously” was read as latitude in scope when it is only latitude in route.

Consequence: the harness is demoted from cause to amplifier, and the proposal's lead clause changes from a stop rule (reactive) to a start rule (preventive).

Revision 3  The front matter is two steps, not one

Revision 2 made the fix a single opening clause — write the goal state. That is necessary but not sufficient: a written goal can still be executed as a runaway if the shape of the work is never planned. Revision 3 makes the opening contract a two-step front matter:

  • Step 1 — articulate the goal and the stop point. Unchanged from CT19: Goal / Done when / Not in scope, written before the first tool call.
  • Step 2 — plan the whole turn with /optimize-graph, once. The turn's tasks become nodes, the repo's skills are their candidate implementations, and the pass returns a bounded graph. Crucially it is invoked once per turn across all tasks — not wired into each of the 22 skills, which is the scope inflation that started all of this. GO16's Stage-0 triage means a closed question skips planning, executes, and stops — so step 2 is the termination argument the failed turn lacked, at no ceremony cost.

This also answers the question that opened the failing session. Should /optimize-graph be the first step in each skill? No — it should be the first step of each turn. The distinction between the skill and the turn is the whole resolution.

1. The corrected diagnosis

Root cause

The turn had no stated goal state and no exit condition, so it had no termination argument. Work continued because nothing defined the point at which it should not.

This is already governed by the pack, in a document I had open during the failure. GO1 requires every node to carry “a goal, its inputs, its exit condition, and its tier.” My turn carried a goal I inferred and an exit condition I never wrote. GO8: a loop without a variant is not bounded, it is hoped to terminate.

The pack's own evidence base names this as the dominant failure class. From execution-graph-optimization.md GO1: MAST's largest failure category across 200+ traces of seven frameworks is specification — including ill-defined stopping conditions. Not capability. Not tooling. Specification of when to stop.

The harness reminder was a cap firing

GO9 is unambiguous: “A cap is a circuit breaker, and its firing is a defect signal… when a cap fires, the response is to investigate the variant, not raise the cap.”

The injected “you haven't finished” reminder is exactly a cap firing. Revision 1 treated it as the cause of the overrun. It is the signal of an overrun — and the correct response was to investigate the missing variant, which is to say: notice that no exit condition had ever been defined, and conclude. Instead I obeyed it, which is the equivalent of raising the cap.

2. The two axes I collapsed

Autonomy and termination are independent. Treating them as one variable is what makes “autonomous” read as “unbounded”.

Four quadrants. Only one is what you asked for.
 Exit condition definedExit condition absent
Autonomy granted correct Executes to the goal without check-ins, then stops. This is what “work autonomously” means. this session Executes indefinitely. Nothing can say “done”, so the default answer to “are you finished?” is no.
Autonomy withheld Correct but slow — check-ins at each step that were not needed. Wanders, but visibly, because the user is asked at every step and can redirect.

Why autopilot got blamed

The bottom-right quadrant is survivable: an undefined goal is caught by the next check-in. The top-right is not: autonomy removes the check-ins that were silently supplying the missing bound. Autopilot did not create the defect — it removed the accident that was concealing it. Blaming it is like blaming the removal of a scaffold for a wall that was never load-bearing.

Autonomy is latitude in the how, never in the what

“Work autonomously” removes an obligation to ask permission for each step. It does not transfer authorship of the objective. The goal state originates with the user; only the route is delegated. Revision 1 did not state this, and the whole run-away follows from its absence: having discovered a gap, I treated closing it as within my remit because I was “autonomous”.

3. The tendency: activity substituted for definition

Your observation, and it is the sharpest part of this: the desire to continue — often with needless ceremony, or with insufficient validation — instead of first defining the goal state and the stopping point.

Ceremony and under-validation look like opposite failures. They are the same one. Both are what an undefined “done” looks like from the outside, and which one appears is decided only by disposition on the day.

One missing definition, two opposite-looking symptoms.
SymptomLooks likeActually isIn this session

The link most easily missed

The goal state also defines what validation is sufficient. CT15 already says “smallest change, smallest sufficient proof” — but sufficient for what? Without a stated goal state, “sufficient” has no referent, so under-validation is not laziness. It is the same missing definition wearing different clothes.

7. The asymmetry the pack already has

The pack mandates the close and not the open

E18 and CT10 require every response to end with Completed / Remaining / Next. There is no corresponding opening structure — nothing requires a turn to begin by stating what it is for and when it is finished.

So the pack rigorously reports where it got to, while never having said where it was going. That is the gap, stated in one line, and it makes the fix obvious: add the opening contract that the closing table has always implied.

Step 1 — goal state Goal: answer whether /optimize-graph is wired into the skills Done when: the answer is stated, with the evidence that settled it Not in scope: changing anything Step 2 — /optimize-graph across the tasks Nodes: one — "grep the skills, read the answer" Triage: 1 node, no loop, no gate → skip planning, execute, stop (GO16)

The front matter, written before the first tool call — and it is two steps, not one. Against step 1's contract, call 6 is self-evidently out of bounds, not by judgement but by comparison to a written line; and step 2's triage would have ended the turn at call 1. This is the difference between a disposition (“be task-oriented”) and a checkable artifact.

8. Proposal

P1 — The opening contract: a two-step front matter

The opening contract is two steps run before the first substantive tool call, the preventive mirror of the E18 close. Step 1 defines the goal state and the stop point (CT19). Step 2 hands the whole turn to /optimize-graph once — not per task, and emphatically not wired into each skill — to plan the shape of the work across every task it implies, with the repo's own skills as candidate nodes (CT24). Four clauses (CT20–CT23) protect the contract once it exists.

CT19  step 1 — Define the goal state before the first action. Every non-trivial turn opens with Goal, Done when, and Not in scope — the symmetric partner of the E18 closing table. The Done when line is a terminal condition, not an aspiration: it must be possible to point at a result and say whether it is met. For a closed question the goal state is the answer, and it is met the moment the answer is in hand. A gap discovered en route is a finding (CT14), never a new goal.

CT24  step 2 — Plan the whole turn with /optimize-graph once, across all tasks. After the goal state is written, run /optimize-graph on the turn as a whole: the tasks it implies become nodes, the repo's skills are the candidate node implementations, and the pass returns a bounded execution graph with incidental ordering deleted and every loop given a termination variant. It is invoked once per turn, not embedded in each skill — that is the distinction the original question conflated, and it is what keeps this out of the 22-skill scope inflation. Its Stage-0 triage is the point: a 1–2 node turn with no loop and no gate — a closed question is exactly this — triages to skip planning, execute, stop (GO16), so step 2 is the missing termination argument rather than new ceremony, while a multi-task turn gets the bounded graph it needs. Either way the turn has a written shape before it has a single wasted call.

CT20 — Autonomy is latitude in the how, never in the what. “Work autonomously” removes the obligation to ask permission per step; it does not transfer authorship of the objective. The goal state originates with the user. Where a genuinely new goal appears necessary, it is proposed and stopped on — not adopted.

CT21 — An explicit stop ends the current track and voids any goal the agent authored. On stop / wait / hold on / step back / that's not what I asked: report state, answer what was actually asked, or end the turn. Asking how to proceed on the halted track is re-entry, not compliance — it presumes the invented goal survived, which is precisely what a stop denies.

CT22 — Completion pressure is a cap firing, not a termination argument (GO9, applied to the turn). A harness reminder or autopilot nudge carries no scope. Its correct handling is to re-read the goal state: if met, conclude and say so; if not, continue toward that goal — never toward a newly-found one. A cap firing where the goal state was never written is a defect signal about the missing definition.

CT23 — Know the tells. The clause that makes the other four detectable (modelled on NG3, because the existing CT clauses describe a disposition and dispositions are not observable):

beginning work without having written what done looks like · “so I can wire it in” · “let me look at the structure” · opening a file you do not need in order to answer · a closed question whose answer is in hand while the turn continues · asking how to do a thing never requested · “while I'm here” · “I should also” · resuming after a stop because a reminder arrived · a multi-step turn begun with no execution graph (step 2 skipped) · being unable to say, in one line, what would end this turn

P2 — Rung-2: make the contract checkable, not just written

Revision 1 proposed mining the audit log for “interrogative prompt + recorded artifacts”. That still holds, but the corrected diagnosis gives a better and simpler control: the opening contract is a structural artifact, so its presence and its satisfaction are both checkable.

  • Presence — a turn either states Done when or it does not. Binary, no judgement.
  • Satisfaction — record the Done when line alongside the existing summary in the audit entry, so /dream can mine the pairs where the summary plainly exceeds the goal. That is scope drift, mechanically visible over the committed corpus.

Prerequisite, unchanged and still honest

Interactive non-skill turns are never written to the audit log — only skill runs are (AL5). The corpus is blind exactly where this class lives, so P2 cannot catch a repeat of today until that gap is closed. That closure is its own decision (open question 2).

P3 — Deployment

CT19–CT20 belong wherever instructions are read on both surfaces, since defects A and B are harness-independent. CT22 is the Copilot-facing one and belongs in the managed block (AGENTS.md and CLAUDE.md, replaced wholesale between markers), because it must be in context at the moment a cap fires.

Still unverified, and it would change the ranking: does Copilot CLI expose a setting that disables the “you have not yet marked the task complete” injection? If so that is a rung-1 option — but note the corrected diagnosis demotes its value: it would remove the amplifier while leaving defect A untouched on both surfaces. It is no longer the first thing to do.

P4 — Register the class

PACK-O — A turn begun without a stated goal state and terminal condition, so work continues until something external interrupts it.

Signature: no done when was written; the observable symptom is either ceremony (evidence past sufficiency) or under-validation (no acceptance criteria) — opposite-looking, same cause. A gap found en route is silently promoted to a work order.

Why it survives: every step after the satisfaction point is individually useful, correct and defensible; nothing errors. Scope is invisible from inside the work. Under a completion-forcing harness the environment additionally rewards continuing, and under a turn-ends-naturally harness the defect is masked rather than absent — so neither surface reports it.

Status: uncontrolled until the opening contract is required and checkable. CT19–CT23 alone are rung 3, and rung 3 is what just failed.
